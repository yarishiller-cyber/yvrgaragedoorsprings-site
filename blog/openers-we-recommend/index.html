Openers we'd buy. If we sold them.

We don't sell openers. We don't install them. But we see a couple hundred a year on spring calls. Here's the short list of what's worth installing in a Lower Mainland house — and what's a coin flip.

Dale McRae
Lead Technician · Vancouver / Burnaby / North Shore / Tri-Cities / Surrey & east

One important thing first: we don't sell openers and we don't install them. When your opener dies and we tell you to call somebody else, this is the list we'd hand you. Take it for what it's worth — we have no financial stake in any of these brands.

The short list

LiftMaster Elite Series belt drive (e.g. 87802) — the default answer

If you have to pick one without thinking, pick this. LiftMaster is owned by the Chamberlain Group, which has been making openers for decades, and it's the brand we see most on Lower Mainland garage ceilings. The Elite belt drives are quiet, well built, and parts are easy to get.

Why we like it: built-in battery backup that gets you through a Lower Mainland windstorm, smartphone control via myQ that pairs reliably, a good light, and very low noise. Best fit for: a standard single or double door with a normal-height ceiling.

Typical installed cost via a real overhead-door contractor: $850 to $1,200 in Greater Vancouver.

LiftMaster 8500C / 8500W (wall-mount) — for vaulted ceilings and high doors

Same Elite family, but the wall-mount jackshaft design: the motor sits on the wall beside the door and turns the torsion shaft directly, so there's no rail on the ceiling at all. (That also means it needs a standard torsion spring setup — which is our department.) If your garage has a 10- or 12-foot ceiling, exposed trusses, or a tall (8-foot+) door, a traditional ceiling-mounted opener is going to struggle with the long rail length. Wall-mount sidesteps the problem entirely.

Common in West Vancouver's British Properties and in rebuilt Vancouver homes with tall garages.

Genie StealthDrive Connect 7155-TKV — the LiftMaster alternative

Genie is one of the biggest opener brands in North America. The StealthDrive Connect is their belt-drive answer to LiftMaster's premium tier. Quiet, smart-home compatible, similar price point. On a lot of Genie models, battery backup is an add-on rather than built in, so check the box.

We see Genies all the time and they're fine. Not better than LiftMaster, not worse. If your house already has a Genie wall console, replacing with another Genie keeps things simple.

Hörmann SupraMatic — the premium European answer

German-engineered, more expensive ($1,400 to $2,000+ installed), and a genuinely nice motor and rail. Soft start and soft stop, easy on the door.

Worth the upcharge if: you have a heavy custom door (cedar carriage, copper-clad), you're staying in the house 20+ years, or you just want the best of the best. Common in West Vancouver British Properties and high-end Shaughnessy rebuilds. Less common everywhere else, partly because the parts distribution network in BC is thinner.

The "fine for the price" tier

Chamberlain (B6753T, B970) — Chamberlain is LiftMaster's residential-retail brand (same parent company, simpler firmware, sold through big-box stores). If your previous opener was a Chamberlain that lasted 15 years, a new Chamberlain will probably do the same. Cheaper than LiftMaster Elite by $200 to $400.

Marantec — German engineering at a less-premium price point than Hörmann. Solid mid-tier (the M-series and Comfort lines). Less common in BC than LiftMaster or Genie, so finding service or parts can be slightly slower.

What we'd avoid

  • Anything with a chain drive in a residential garage. Loud, dated, and the price difference vs. a belt-drive is small now. The exception is a detached garage where noise doesn't matter.
  • The cheapest big-box-store opener under $250. The motor's underpowered, the drive gear wears fast, the rail is flimsy. You'll be replacing it again in 5 years.
  • Anything without battery backup if you live in North Van, West Van, up on Burke Mountain, or anywhere with big trees over the power lines. Winter windstorms knock the power out regularly. Manual release works, but you're lifting the door by hand at 6 a.m. in the rain.
  • "Smart" openers that require a paid subscription to use the app. (Some brands put features like video history or third-party integrations behind a paid plan.) Read the fine print.

What matters more than the brand

  1. Belt drive over chain drive. Quieter, longer-lasting, less maintenance. Worth the $50–$100 premium.
  2. Battery backup. Built into the LiftMaster Elite belt drives, often an add-on elsewhere. Lower Mainland has enough power outages that this matters.
  3. Photo-eye sensors that align easily. Most modern brands are fine here, but the cheap ones have finicky alignment that drifts.
  4. A good installer. The opener itself is half the job. The rail, the up/down limits, the force settings, and checking the door is balanced before the opener goes on — that's the other half. It's not the plug-and-play job the box makes it look like. Pay for a real overhead-door contractor.

What to do when your opener dies

First — confirm it's actually the opener and not the spring. We've got a 30-second diagnostic for that. If the door is heavy in your hand when you lift it manually, the spring is the problem, not the opener.

If it's confirmed-opener: call an overhead-door contractor (not a handyman, not a general home-services company). Look for an established company with a business licence, insurance and WorkSafeBC coverage, and ask whether they check the door's balance before they hang the opener. Steveston Garage Doors does openers too. We don't get a kickback for any referral.

If your spring is the problem, the closest tech is typically 12–18 minutes away.
