Custom carriage doors and triple garages are the daily work here.

West Vancouver runs different. British Properties is 3- and 4-car garages with oversized wood-clad carriage doors, often custom-built with copper accents and heavier-than-standard weight. Marine Drive estates have similar setups plus direct salt spray. Your West Van tech stocks heavier-gauge springs and longer lengths than the standard residential build.

We see a lot of LiftMaster Elite and Hörmann premium openers up here — they're more torque-capable than basic chain-drive, which means they can mask a failing spring for longer before the whole system gives up. By the time the opener strains, the spring may already be cracked. Don't wait for the opener to refuse — listen for the bang.

British Properties elevation gets snow and ice multiple weeks per winter. Caulfeild and Ambleside oceanfront homes get salt spray. Both wear out hardware faster than central Vancouver. High-cycle springs and stainless cables are the right spec for most West Van rebuilds.

West Van questions

The ones we get from this city.

I have a 20-foot copper-clad carriage door on a British Properties triple garage. Quote?

Heavier than standard residential, so heavier-gauge springs at a parts upcharge. We'd measure on site and quote you before we wind anything. Most triple-garage carriage doors land in the $1,400 to $1,800 range all-in.

My LiftMaster Elite still works fine. Why did my spring still snap?

The opener masks spring fatigue until the spring fully cracks. A premium opener can lift a partially-failed spring system for months without you noticing — until it can't. The bang is the metal finishing what it started.

Caulfeild — direct ocean exposure. Stainless springs worth it?

For oceanfront homes, yes. Standard oil-tempered will go 8–12 years here vs. 12–18 inland. Stainless adds parts cost but doesn't pit and stays smooth in salt spray. Ask your tech for the stainless option.

British Properties — steep driveways, snow days. Does my opener need work too?

Possibly. If the opener is straining after a fresh spring install, it's probably the opener motor or the rail — different job. We'll diagnose, but we don't sell openers. We'll refer you to a trusted local opener guy if needed.
