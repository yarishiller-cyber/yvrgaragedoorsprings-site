Terra Nova, Hamilton, and the slow sinking of Lulu Island — how delta silt and salt air kill TorqueMaster springs in Richmond.

Richmond sits about a metre above sea level. The ground under it is soft delta silt that's still slowly settling, and the water table is high year-round. None of that is the headline failure mode, though. The headline is what salt-laden onshore winds and damp air do to a TorqueMaster spring sealed inside a steel tube. Terra Nova, Hamilton, and the older parts of Cambie are full of 1990s and 2000s builds running TorqueMasters, and in our experience they're wearing out years ahead of their rated cycle count.

Madison Lim
Lead Technician · Richmond / Delta / Tsawwassen / White Rock / South Surrey

Why Richmond is different from the rest of Metro Vancouver

Three factors stack against your garage door hardware in Richmond, and they all compound:

First, the salt. Steveston sits right at the mouth of the Fraser, and the west side of Richmond looks straight out over Sturgeon Bank to the Strait of Georgia. Onshore winds carry salt across the western half of Lulu Island, and it settles on every steel surface — your patio furniture, the chain on your bike, and the inside of your TorqueMaster tube. Steel simply corrodes faster in coastal air than it does inland. In our experience, a TorqueMaster spring that should last 6-7 years gets 4-5 on the west side of Richmond.

Second, the humidity. Lulu Island's water table is high year-round, and pretty much the whole island — Hamilton and East Cambie included — sits on saturated silt. Fall and winter fog can hang around well into the day. Inside a TorqueMaster tube, moisture has nowhere to go: the tube traps damp air around the spring and it dries out far slower than the garage around it.

Third, the settling. Parts of the Fraser delta are sinking by a millimetre or two a year, and houses on soft ground shift a little as they age. It isn't enough to break anything, but over twenty-odd years a slab and a door opening can move slightly out of true. A door that rubs or binds in its tracks makes the springs work harder on every cycle, and that eats into their life.

Terra Nova: the late-'90s build wave

Terra Nova, in Richmond's northwest corner, was mostly farmland until the late 1990s. Most of its detached houses went up over the following decade or so, and a lot of them got Wayne Dalton doors with the TorqueMaster Original. Plenty of the ones we see are now on their second or third spring set, and that's where the parts-availability wall hits.

Wayne Dalton stopped making TorqueMaster Original parts years ago. For a Terra Nova home with a broken Original, the only paths are a TorqueMaster Plus conversion kit (proprietary parts, same enclosed-tube design, same corrosion trap) or a full standard-torsion conversion (universal parts, high-cycle springs available, and any spring shop in BC can service it next time).

Hamilton and Cambie: the TorqueMaster Plus years

A lot of the Hamilton and Cambie subdivisions we work in came a little later, which puts many of those homes in TorqueMaster Plus territory. Plus is the better generation, but "better" doesn't beat the moisture problem. A Hamilton home out at the east end of the island gets far less salt than Steveston does, but it sits beside the river on the same wet silt, and the damp inside the tube still does its work.

The Plus has an anti-drop safety feature, which is a genuine improvement: if a spring breaks while the door is open, the door locks in place instead of free-falling. But the spring still breaks. The anti-drop doesn't extend cycle life; it just makes the failure safer. You still need the service call.

What we install on a Richmond conversion

Two-spring high-cycle torsion is our default Richmond recommendation. The high-cycle tier (flat-rate, all-in) gets you 25,000-cycle rated oil-tempered springs (2.5× the standard 10,000-cycle TorqueMaster spec), which in salt air hold up better than galvanized springs whose coating wears off where the coils rub. Sealed bearings instead of open bushings, too, which last much longer in this humidity.

For homes within about a kilometre of the west dike — west Steveston, Westwind, the western edge of Terra Nova — we'll quote stainless cables as an upgrade. $40-60 in parts, and noticeably longer cable life when the door breathes ocean air. Most Richmond customers don't need it; west-edge customers usually do.

The conversion itself takes 90 minutes to 2 hours on a 16×7 double-car door. We remove the entire TorqueMaster tube, install a 1-inch torsion shaft, end bearing plates, the centre support, and the new spring pair. Cables and drums get replaced as a matter of course because the existing ones are 15-25 years old and full of micro-rust.
