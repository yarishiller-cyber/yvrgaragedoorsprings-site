Westwood Plateau's TorqueMaster crisis — why a 1990s luxury showcase is calling us on Sunday afternoons.

Westwood Plateau was the showcase Coquitlam wanted in the 1990s — built where the old Westwood racing circuit used to run, anchored by a golf course, and filled with big executive homes through the '90s and early 2000s. A large share of them, behind those double and triple garages, got Wayne Dalton 9100 doors with TorqueMaster Originals. Those springs are now hitting end of life, all at once.

Dale McRae
Lead Technician · Vancouver / Burnaby / North Shore / Tri-Cities / Surrey & east

Why every house up here has the same spring system

Production homebuilders in the 1990s loved TorqueMaster for one reason: install speed. A standard torsion spring needs a tech with winding bars, time to set the tension, and a balance test. A TorqueMaster came pre-matched to the door — bolt up the tube, fit the ends, wind it with a power drill, done in about 20 minutes. For a builder closing several homes a week on the Plateau, the math was obvious.

So the big builders up here mostly spec'd the Wayne Dalton 9100 with a TorqueMaster Original as the standard fit on the 16×7 double door. The larger homes along streets like Plateau Boulevard and Panorama Drive often have triple garages — a double door and a single, each with its own TorqueMaster tube. Same system, twice the failure surface.

What's breaking, and why now

The math is unforgiving. TorqueMaster Originals were rated for the industry-standard 10,000 cycles. Field reality: in a Westwood Plateau home with three vehicles going through one door three or four times a day, you hit 10,000 cycles in about seven years. A house built in 1996 has been through two or three spring sets by now. Whatever's up there today was installed by whoever did the last service — sometimes correctly, sometimes not.

And the breaks cluster on weekends. A Sunday of errands, hockey drop-offs, and three cars in and out is exactly the kind of day that finishes off a tired spring — which is why so many Plateau calls come in on Sunday afternoon.

Elevation makes it worse. Much of the Plateau sits a few hundred metres above the Coquitlam flats, and from November through March it gets frosty mornings the lower neighbourhoods rarely see. Cold steel is less forgiving. A spring with 1,500 cycles left at 12°C might give up at −4°C on a Tuesday morning when someone hits the opener on the way to school drop-off. The break is sudden and complete: one bang you can hear from the kitchen, the door won't open, and the opener strains and quits.

Then there's the Original's gear problem. The plastic winding gears in the TorqueMaster end brackets were meant for a drill at moderate torque. Plenty of homeowners — and some installers — used impact drivers, which can strip the teeth in one pass. We saw four stripped Original gear assemblies on the Plateau in a single month this spring. Original parts have been discontinued for years. There is no replacement.

What we actually do when we get there

Two paths: stay with Wayne Dalton (a TorqueMaster Plus retrofit kit), or convert to standard torsion. We almost always recommend converting.

Why: standard torsion is universal. Any garage door shop in BC can service it, and the springs are off-the-shelf parts rather than a special order from a Wayne Dalton dealer. You can also go to 25,000-cycle high-cycle springs — 2.5× the rated life of a 10,000-cycle TorqueMaster. On a Plateau home with 25 years of TorqueMaster repairs behind it, skip the in-kind swap: your next service should be a $100 standard part, not a $250 special order.

The conversion on a 16×7 9100 takes about two hours. We take out the whole TorqueMaster tube and install a 1-inch steel shaft, end bearing plates, a centre support bearing, and conventional torsion springs sized to the door's actual weight. Cables and drums get replaced as a matter of course — after 25-plus years, the originals are usually worn or corroded.

The Plateau-specific spec we recommend

For Westwood Plateau homes — especially on the higher streets — we recommend the high-cycle tier (flat-rate, all-in) over the standard two-spring tier. The freeze-thaw up there is real, and a 25,000-cycle spring has far more fatigue headroom at −8°C than a 10,000-cycle one. The modest premium pays for itself in years 7 through 12 — exactly when a standard spring would be giving up.

If the door is anywhere near a furnace vent or dryer exhaust, we swap the standard open bushings for sealed bearings. Plenty of Plateau floor plans put the laundry room on the garage wall, which pushes humid air toward the spring hardware every time the dryer runs. Sealed bearings cost us $40 in parts and roughly double bearing life.

Where your house sits on the timeline

Built in the 1990s: you're probably on your third spring set, on a TorqueMaster Original with no parts left. Built in the 2000s: you may have an early TorqueMaster Plus on its second set, and it's due. You can replace it on your own schedule or wait for the bang — the price is the same flat rate either way, but only one of those options leaves your car stuck in the garage on a Sunday.
