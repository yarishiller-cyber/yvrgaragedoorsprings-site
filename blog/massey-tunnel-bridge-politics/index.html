The Massey Tunnel and your spring job: a candid map of South Arm response times.

Every Delta, Tsawwassen, and South Surrey customer I've had since 2009 has asked me the same thing in the first ninety seconds of the call: "Where are you coming from?" The reason they ask is the Massey Tunnel, and the reason the Massey Tunnel is the question is that we have been arguing about replacing it for well over a decade.

Madison Lim
Lead Technician · Richmond / Delta / Tsawwassen / White Rock / South Surrey

This post is partly about garage door response times. It's mostly about how we get a tech to the south side of the river without betting your afternoon on the tunnel, and what the Massey replacement means for those of you in Tsawwassen, Ladner, South Surrey and White Rock.

Spoiler: it matters. And after more than a decade of back-and-forth, a replacement is finally being built.

What the tunnel actually is

The George Massey Tunnel opened in 1959. Four lanes, with counterflow to give the rush-hour direction an extra lane, running under the South Arm of the Fraser between Richmond and Delta. We are now in year 67. It carries somewhere around 80,000 vehicles a day and has been flagged as a bottleneck in provincial planning documents for as long as I've been driving it. Nobody who drives it would disagree.

The replacement saga goes like this, briefly: the Clark government proposed a 10-lane bridge. The Horgan government shelved it after an independent review and chose an 8-lane immersed-tube tunnel instead. The Eby government has kept that plan. Work is under way, and the province has pointed to around 2030 for opening — I'll believe the date when I drive it. Until then, the existing tunnel is what we have. And the existing tunnel is what shapes a south-of-the-river morning.

The reason these numbers matter to you isn't that I'm complaining — it's that they explain how we dispatch. Every call goes to the technician closest to you. A company that sends one truck out from across town is sitting in the same traffic you'd be sitting in, except they're billing you for it and we're not.

Where I'm based, and who crosses the river

I live in Steveston and mostly work Richmond. From home, a Steveston call is about 4 minutes and central Richmond is 8 to 15. But Ladner and Tsawwassen are on the other side of the South Arm, which means the tunnel — so we don't lean on me for those. We have techs based south of the river too, and the call goes to whoever is closest. Most Delta and Tsawwassen jobs never go near the tunnel.

The reason the local-tech model matters so much down here is that the river is a moat. If you're a Vancouver-based contractor and you tell a Tsawwassen homeowner you'll be there at 10 a.m., you're really telling them you'll be there at 11:15 if the tunnel has a fender bender. Closest-tech dispatch exists because the tunnel exists.

The Alex Fraser as an alternative

The Alex Fraser Bridge — Highway 91, crossing from Annacis Island to North Delta — is the other way over. It's a longer route from central Richmond, and it has its own bad days, but it usually moves better than the tunnel. For a North Delta or Surrey run from Richmond, I take the Alex Fraser by default. For Ladner and Tsawwassen, the Massey is the natural route because it drops me straight onto Highway 17.

The thing nobody tells newer residents: it's all one bottleneck. When the tunnel slows down, drivers bail to Highway 91 and the Alex Fraser, and the backups spread onto the Oak Street and Knight Street bridges on the way to and from Vancouver. By 4 p.m. it's the same jam wearing different highway numbers. Dale has a whole separate rant about the Pattullo.

What the new tunnel will actually do

The new crossing is an 8-lane immersed-tube tunnel — same basic method as the old one, but with twice the lanes and modern fire-safety and seismic standards. (The current Massey was built long before anyone was designing for the Big One. That is not a small thing.)

Construction means lane closures and detours for years. Once it opens, it should ease the worst of the rush-hour crawl between Delta and Richmond. For us, it means an easier trip for whichever tech does have to cross. Until then, the answer is still "have a tech already on the south side."

The Tsawwassen / Delta dispatch reality

Here's what I tell every south-of-the-Fraser customer who calls:

  • If you're in Ladner, Tsawwassen, Beach Grove, English Bluff, or North Delta — the closest tech is usually 12 to 18 minutes away, and they won't be coming through the tunnel.
  • If you're in White Rock or South Surrey — same deal. The call goes to a tech already south of the river.
  • If you're in central Richmond — that's usually me, 8 to 15 minutes from Steveston.
  • If you're north of the North Arm — Dale or another tech closer to you takes the call. Same crew, same number.

None of these numbers assume the tunnel is empty. They assume the tunnel is normal. If the tunnel is closed for a stalled truck, Delta and Tsawwassen response times barely move, because the tech coming to you isn't going through it. That is the entire reason we dispatch this way.

What you should know if you're calling from south of the river

Start with your closest cross street. We'll need the address to book, but the cross street tells me most of what I need to know. The South Arm is hard to explain unless you grew up on it: if you say "56th Street and 12th Avenue in Tsawwassen," I can tell you whether your hardware needs stainless cables before you've finished your sentence. The neighbourhood predicts the air. The air predicts the spec.

Same crew. Same number. The old tunnel's days are numbered; the new one will outlive all of us. In the meantime, call — whoever's closest is on the way, tunnel or no tunnel.
